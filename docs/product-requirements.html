Executive summary

What ships, who it serves, and how to tell if it works.

→
Product promise

Open the app, begin a calm four-phase session in two taps, or set each phase independently—for example 4–4–6–4. Finish without watching the screen. With permission, the session and any available biometric samples are connected to Apple Health or Health Connect.

Primary user

The stressed beginner

Wants a quick, private way to calm down without learning a meditation system or committing to a subscription.

Secondary user

The regular practitioner

Already knows box breathing and wants adjustable timing, reliable cues, and health-app logging.

Jobs to be done

  • When I feel stressed, help me start a guided breathing cycle immediately.
  • When I cannot watch the screen, guide each transition with sound or haptics.
  • When I complete a practice, let me record it alongside my other wellness activity.
  • When compatible wearable data is available, help me see simple relationships between practice, heart rate, recovery indicators, sleep, and consistency—without medical claims.
  • When I return, remember my preferred four phase timings and cue settings on this device.

MVP success signals

Activation

≥ 60%

of first-time users start a session after onboarding.

Completion

≥ 70%

of started sessions reach the final cycle.

Reliability

≥ 99.5%

crash-free sessions during launch validation.

Targets are initial product hypotheses, not market benchmarks. Revisit after the first 500 opted-in usage sessions.

04

Functional requirements

Testable behavior for the product and engineering team.

FR-01 · Session configuration
  • Home shows current duration plus inhale, first-hold, exhale, and second-hold values without opening another screen.
  • Each phase is independently adjustable from 1–20 seconds using accessible steppers; 4–4–6–4 is a valid profile.
  • Changing any value immediately updates cycle length, estimated cycle count, and guided breaths per minute.
  • Selections persist locally and restore on the next launch; invalid or free-text values cannot be entered.
FR-02 · Timer accuracy
  • Use a monotonic clock and calculate phase from elapsed time; never rely on chained one-second intervals.
  • Visual countdown updates smoothly while tone, voice, or haptic cues fire once per phase boundary.
  • After interruption or app resume, reconcile against elapsed wall time and show the correct phase.
  • A five-minute foreground session finishes within ±250 ms of its intended duration in device tests.
FR-03 · Guidance cues
  • The closed beta includes distinct, non-verbal tones for inhale, hold, exhale, and completion, plus independently toggleable haptics.
  • Use beta feedback to validate cue timing, clarity, loudness, and whether guidance works without watching the screen before recording voice assets.
  • Before public launch, add professionally recorded, concise phase prompts synchronized to the same timer boundaries. Do not use synthetic speech.
  • At launch, users can choose tones or recorded voice, and toggle haptics independently; the visual-only path remains available.
  • Haptic patterns are recognizable without being startling; unsupported devices degrade silently.
  • Respect silent-mode and audio-focus conventions per platform; do not unexpectedly interrupt other audio.
FR-04 · Interruption handling
  • Incoming call, audio interruption, or app backgrounding pauses guidance unless platform-safe background behavior is explicitly implemented and tested.
  • Returning shows a clear paused state, never an apparently active but stale timer.
  • Force-quit during a session does not create a completed record.
  • Screen can remain awake during active guidance when the user permits; normal sleep behavior resumes afterward.
FR-05 · Session history
  • Only completed or intentionally ended sessions are stored.
  • Each entry includes start time, actual duration, all four phase values, completed cycles, guided cadence, completion state, health-write status, and available biometric summaries.
  • User can delete all local history from Settings after an in-page confirmation step.
  • Deleting local history does not delete records already written to Apple Health or Health Connect; explain this before deletion.
FR-06 · Offline behavior
  • Every breathing feature works in airplane mode after install.
  • No account, network request, remote content, or server is required to begin or complete a session.
  • Health writes queue locally if the platform API is temporarily unavailable and retry on the next app open.
FR-07 · Safety language
  • Onboarding says to stop and return to normal breathing if lightheaded or uncomfortable.
  • Settings includes a durable wellness—not medical advice—statement.
  • No copy promises to treat anxiety, disease, insomnia, or any medical condition.
  • No forced breath holds longer than the selected count and no competitive scoring.
  • Heart rate, HRV, resting heart rate, respiratory rate, SpO₂, and sleep are presented as recorded wellness data—not diagnoses, readiness scores, causal claims, or treatment guidance.
FR-08 · Wearable health reads
  • Use Apple HealthKit on iOS and Health Connect on Android as the only wearable-data aggregators; do not add direct Fitbit, Garmin, Oura, Samsung, or other vendor SDKs in MVP.
  • Request read access for each supported type only after explaining its use: heart rate, HRV, resting heart rate, respiratory rate, blood oxygen saturation (SpO₂), and sleep.
  • Associate timestamped heart-rate samples whose timestamps overlap the actual session window; do not synthesize or carry forward missing readings.
  • Show heart rate during practice only when a compatible source supplies a recent sample; otherwise omit the value without interrupting guidance.
  • Guided breath cadence is derived from the selected phase profile and labeled “guided cadence,” never “measured breathing rate.”
FR-09 · Insights & progression
  • Provide 7-day and 30-day trend views for resting heart rate, HRV, and mindful minutes; clearly label the selected date range and units.
  • On each qualifying session summary, compare average heart rate from a defined pre-session window with average heart rate from a defined post-session window; show the windows and sample sufficiency.
  • Aggregate completed-session count and total minutes by timing profile or named breathing pattern.
  • Show current and longest practice streaks based on local calendar days, with timezone changes handled deterministically.
  • Use neutral descriptions such as “higher,” “lower,” or “no clear change”; never generate a readiness score, diagnosis, causal claim, or treatment recommendation.
NFR-01 · Graceful data absence
  • Every timer, cue, history, and session-completion function works with no wearable, denied permissions, or no health records.
  • The Insights screen shows an empty state explaining that availability depends on connected devices and granted permissions, with a route to connection guidance.
  • Missing one data type hides only the affected chart or comparison; it must not blank the entire Insights screen.
  • Delayed wearable sync updates insights on a later app open without rewriting or duplicating the breathing session.
05

Health integration

Record the practice, add available sensor context, and make denial or missing hardware fully supported states.

iOS aggregator

Apple HealthKit

  • Write completed mindful sessions.
  • Request read access, per type, for heart rate, HRV, resting heart rate, respiratory rate, SpO₂, and sleep.
  • Use records contributed by Apple Watch or any other source the user has connected to Apple Health.
  • Do not integrate wearable-vendor SDKs directly.
Android aggregator

Health Connect

  • Write the supported breathing or mindfulness session record.
  • Request read access, per type, for heart rate, HRV, resting heart rate, respiratory rate, SpO₂, and sleep.
  • Use records contributed by compatible watches and apps that the user has connected to Health Connect.
  • Do not integrate Fitbit, Garmin, Samsung, or other vendor SDKs directly.

Supported health indicators

During practice
The app records the phase profile, cycle timestamps, completed cycles, and guided cadence. With permission, it associates heart-rate samples that overlap the session and may show a recent reading when one exists.
Heart & recovery
Heart rate supports session context and before-vs-after comparison. Resting heart rate and HRV support 7-day and 30-day trend views. Keep platform units and metric definitions explicit.
Breathing & oxygen
Respiratory rate and blood oxygen saturation (SpO₂) appear only when the platform contains records from a compatible source. Do not infer either value from the timer.
Sleep
Read permitted sleep sessions and duration for contextual display. MVP does not score sleep, infer sleep quality, or claim that breathing practice caused a change.
Mindful minutes
Trend completed breathing minutes over 7 and 30 days, combining the app’s local sessions with platform records only where duplicate prevention is reliable.
Availability
Data varies by connected devices, source-app sync behavior, sampling frequency, platform support, and permissions. The phone alone may provide none of these measurements.
≠
Guidance is not measurement

The timer knows the rhythm it instructed; it does not know whether the user followed each breath. Heart, breathing, oxygen, HRV, resting heart rate, and sleep values appear only when Apple Health or Health Connect provides real records.

Permission flow

  1. User completes the first qualifying session.
  2. Completion offers “Add sessions to Health.” Insights separately explains the benefit of each read category before requesting access.
  3. Open the native permission sheet and let the user grant or deny every supported type. Never simulate consent or treat partial access as all-or-nothing.
  4. Write the completed session; query only granted data types for the exact ranges needed by session comparison and 7-day/30-day insights.
  5. On denial or missing data, keep the full breathing experience and show a neutral Insights empty state with connection guidance.

Health acceptance criteria

  • One completed app session creates at most one health record; retries are idempotent.
  • Sessions under 60 seconds are not written.
  • Every displayed indicator names its unit, date range, and availability state: recent, imported, unavailable, or permission denied.
  • No value is carried forward, interpolated, or fabricated when records are missing.
  • Charts remain correct under partial permissions—for example HRV allowed but sleep denied.
  • Changing device time or crossing midnight does not corrupt session duration, streaks, or sample matching.
  • Tests cover no wearable, delayed sync, sparse records, mixed data sources, permission revocation, and supported data on physical devices.

A cross-platform foundation with native health bridges and no backend dependency.

Framework

React Native with Expo development builds and TypeScript. Expo Go alone is insufficient for native health modules.

Expo Router or React Navigation; keep a shallow stack with modal setup and settings screens.

None for MVP. Optional privacy-preserving product analytics can be added only with explicit policy disclosure.

Timer model

Data & privacy

Collect less, explain clearly, and keep sensitive wellness data under user control.

Local data model

Session
id, timestamps, duration, four-value phaseProfile, optional pattern name, guided cadence, cycles, completion state, health sync state, and optional before/after heart-rate summary.
Progression
Derived 7-day/30-day series for resting heart rate, HRV, and mindful minutes; totals by pattern; current and longest streak. Recompute from local sessions and permitted platform records.
Preferences
Duration, four independent phase seconds, guidance mode (tones or recorded voice at launch), haptics, reduced motion override, reminder setting, onboarding complete, and per-data-type permission/availability state.
Wearable data
Keep raw heart rate, HRV, resting heart rate, respiratory rate, SpO₂, and sleep records in Apple Health or Health Connect. Cache only bounded summaries needed for responsive insights, with source and date-range metadata.
Not stored
Name, email, precise location, contacts, advertising ID, free-text journal, diagnosis, readiness score, or a duplicate long-term store of raw wearable records.

Privacy requirements

  • Publish a plain-language privacy policy before beta distribution.
  • Session write and each read type—heart rate, HRV, resting heart rate, respiratory rate, SpO₂, and sleep—are explained, optional, revocable, and never used for advertising.
  • Do not sell or broker session data. Do not place third-party ad SDKs in the MVP.
  • If analytics is enabled, use coarse product events without health values, exact timestamps, or persistent cross-app identifiers.
  • Provide “Delete local history” and explain the separate platform steps for deleting health records.
  • Document every SDK and its data behavior for App Store privacy labels and Google Play Data Safety.

08

Quality bar

Wellness UX fails when timing, cues, or accessibility are unreliable.

Performance
  • Cold launch to usable home under 2 seconds on target devices.
  • 60 fps breathing animation on mid-range devices.
  • No network dependency for core flow.
Accessibility
  • WCAG AA contrast.
  • Dynamic text without clipping.
  • VoiceOver/TalkBack phase announcements.
  • Reduced-motion alternative.
Reliability
  • No duplicate health records.
  • No stale or fabricated wearable values.
  • Full breathing flow with no wearable data.
  • Crash-free session target ≥99.5%.
  • Deterministic timer and trend tests.

Device test matrix

iOS
Oldest supported iOS version on a smaller iPhone; current iOS on standard and large-screen iPhones; VoiceOver; silent mode; reduced motion.
Android
Oldest supported Android version, one mid-range physical device, current Pixel, one Samsung device; TalkBack; battery saver; Health Connect unavailable and installed states.
Wearables
No wearable, Apple Health/Health Connect unavailable, delayed sync, sparse or mixed-source records, every combination of per-type permission granted/denied/revoked, and records arriving after completion.
Interruptions
Call, alarm, audio route change, app background/foreground, screen lock, permission revocation, low battery, timezone change.

Release blockers

  • Timer or phase cue drifts by more than one second.
  • App records duplicate, zero-length, or incorrect-time health sessions.
  • App labels guided cadence as measured respiratory rate, shows stale data as current, invents a missing wearable value, or presents an insight as a medical conclusion.
  • No-wearable or partial-permission users cannot complete a session or reach a useful Insights empty/partial state.
  • Session cannot be paused or safely ended.
  • Core guidance is unusable with screen reader or reduced motion.
  • Any health permission is requested before context is explained.
  • Privacy policy and store disclosures disagree with actual SDK behavior.

Delivery plan

A realistic sequence for one experienced cross-platform engineer plus part-time design and QA.

Week 1

Product + interaction foundation

Confirm MVP decisions, prototype timer states, finalize copy and safety language, establish app project and CI.

Weeks 2–3

Core breathing experience

Home, timer engine, animation, non-verbal tones, haptics, pause/resume, local preferences, and completion state. No recorded voice in this build.

Week 4

History + progression

Local repository, history, pattern totals, streak logic, 7-day/30-day views, empty states, dynamic text, screen reader flow, and reduced motion.

Week 5

Native health integrations

HealthKit and Health Connect session writes, six permission-gated read types, before/after heart-rate windows, trend queries, deduplication, and physical-device verification.

Week 6

Hardening

Variable-phase timers, trend calculations, streaks, partial permissions, no-watch and delayed-sync cases, lifecycle testing, privacy review, and polish.

Week 7

Tone + haptic closed beta

TestFlight and Play closed testing with 20–50 users. Validate cue timing, comprehension, volume, haptic comfort, starts, completions, and device-specific failures before recording voice.

Week 8

Recorded voice launch candidate

Resolve beta cue issues, finalize and record concise voice scripts, add the voice guidance option, and test timing, accessibility, audio focus, bundled assets, and fallback to tones.

Weeks 9–10

Store submission

Run release-candidate regression, prepare listings and screenshots, complete disclosures, and submit both platforms only after recorded voice guidance passes launch checks.

This is a planning estimate, not a fixed delivery promise. Native health edge cases and store review feedback are the largest schedule risks.

Team

Minimum build team

1 cross-platform mobile engineer, product owner, and a designer/QA partner available part-time.

Helpful review

Privacy/legal review for health wording and a qualified pranayama instructor to review cue language and safety—not to create medical claims.

Launch checklist

Definition of done for the public MVP.

Product

  • All must-have scope passes acceptance tests.
  • The tones-and-haptics build completes closed-beta testing before voice scripts are finalized.
  • Recorded voice prompts are professionally produced, reviewed for clarity, and included in the public launch build.
  • Onboarding and first session tested with new users.
  • Empty, loading, denied, unavailable, and error states have final copy.
  • No unfinished controls, sample history, or dead links.

Engineering

  • Production signing, CI builds, crash reporting, and versioning configured.
  • Tone, recorded voice, haptic, and visual-only guidance paths stay synchronized and work offline.
  • Session writes and all six read categories verified on physical iOS and Android devices, including no-wearable and partial-permission cases.
  • Offline and lifecycle tests pass.
  • Dependency and permission audit completed.

Store readiness

  • App name, subtitle, descriptions, icon, screenshots, and support URL prepared.
  • Privacy policy URL and contact channel live.
  • Apple privacy labels and Google Play Data Safety answers match the app.
  • Age rating, wellness disclaimer, and review notes complete.

Go / no-go

  • No release blocker from the quality section remains.
  • Closed beta has at least 7 days of stable usage.
  • Health-denied users can complete every core flow.
  • Rollback and hotfix owner is named.
