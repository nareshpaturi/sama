01 / Why refresh

The current mark says the wrong things.

The pine field, the sky and coral palette, and the coral point are strong. The drawing inside them works against the brand now that Viram is practitioner-first.

  1. It reads as a leaf.

    The drop, pointed at the top with a central stroke, is read as a leaf with veins, and the brand guide rules out leaf and lotus imagery.

  2. The strokes form a double-barred cross.

    A cross with two bars resembles the Cross of Lorraine, the long-standing emblem of tuberculosis and lung-health charities, as well as a medical cross. For a breathing practice, that points toward illness.

  3. Nothing says breath or balance.

    A drop suggests water. The name's meaning, even and balanced, isn't expressed anywhere in the mark.

  4. The detail breaks down small.

    At 29 px the inner strokes blur into a smudge; the coral point is the only element that survives.

Keep: the pine field, sky, mist, and coral; the coral point as the brand's signature; Newsreader for the wordmark. Replace: the drop and the cross.

02 / Logo concepts

Four ways to draw an even breath.

Each concept keeps the pine field and the coral point, and is shown on pine, in one color, and at 60, 40, and 29 px. Concept A is recommended.

A · Two breaths

Recommended

Two equal arcs make one open circle: inhale and exhale, left and right. The coral point rests in the gap at the top, the pause between breaths. The open bottom is the rest.

Strengths
  • Expresses balance: two equal halves
  • Echoes Nadi Shodhana's left and right
  • Not a leaf, lotus, cross, or yin-yang
  • Clear at 29 px
Watch
  • Can read as parentheses without the coral point
  • Keep the gaps generous so it never closes into a ring

B · Even wave

Alternative

One breath as a single wave with an equal crest and trough, led by the coral point. Rhythm and evenness, drawn as a line.

Strengths
  • Instantly reads as rhythm
  • Very simple to reproduce
Watch
  • Common in audio, music, and fitness apps
  • Horizontal shape leaves the square field half empty

C · The guide

Alternative

The practice screen as a symbol: the breathing guide, its progress ring, and the coral point marking where you are.

Strengths
  • True to the product: the icon is what you follow
  • Strong small-size silhouette
Watch
  • Circles are the category default
  • Can read as a loading spinner or timer

D · Monogram

Alternative

The Newsreader S from the wordmark, with the coral point at its upper terminal. Typographic, quiet, and closely tied to the name.

Strengths
  • Pairs naturally with the wordmark
  • Easy to own with a custom-drawn S
Watch
  • Says nothing about breath
  • Single letters are crowded on home screens (Spotify, Skype, Slack)

Viram
Splash

Paper background, tile mark, wordmark below.

  • Grid100 × 100; circle of radius 28 centered at (50, 52), slightly low so the coral point balances the weight.
  • ArcsStroke 9 with round caps; 28° gaps at the top and bottom, measured from the center. The gaps must never close.
  • Coral pointRadius 4.6, centered in the top gap. It is the only coral in the mark.
  • ColorLeft arc sky #A8CFD0, right arc mist #EEF4EF, point coral #E46F51, field pine #12372F. One-color versions use pine or paper throughout.
  • Minimum size16 px with the field; 24 px without it. Clear space: one quarter of the mark's width.
  • Don'tRotate it, close the gaps, fill the circle, color the arcs to match, or add a lotus, leaf, or glow.

In the construction drawing the right arc is shown in a darker tone for visibility on mist; the production mark uses mist on pine. Also tested and set aside: tapered halves (elegant, but they read as serif parentheses and thin out below 40 px), a point at the center (reads as a record button or target), and a single top gap (reads as a power button).

04 / Tagline

Speak to the practice, not the settings.

05 / Style

Refine the style you have.

Three directions were compared against the practitioner-first positioning. The recommendation is to keep Pine & Paper, which is already implemented and contrast-tested, and to codify the parts the new features need.

Pine & Paper

Recommended · current

Calm, literate, botanical without leaves. Newsreader and DM Sans. Distinct from Calm's blues, Headspace's orange, and Breathwrk's black.

Risk: can feel reserved; the new illustration and cue rules add warmth.

Nadi ShodhanaAlternate nostril breathingBegin · 5 min

Monsoon Earth

Alternative

Indigo, turmeric, clay, and rice paper; a warmer serif such as Fraunces. More visibly rooted in India.

Risk: slides toward spice-market clichés; forces a full re-theme and new contrast work.

Nadi ShodhanaAlternate nostril breathingBegin · 5 min

Clear Air

Alternative

White, ink, one cool accent, and a sans-only type system. Crisp and modern.

Risk: reads as a utility or fitness tool, the opposite of “faithful”; close to Breathwrk.

What the refined style adds

Line diagrams

Technique diagrams use one 1.8 px pine line, paper and mist fills, and plain labels. Mirror view, no faces or bodies, no decoration.

Icons

24 px grid, 1.7 px rounded strokes, no fills. Breathe uses the wave from the mark's language.

Motion

One gentle ease that follows the breath, no overshoot. The coral point is the only thing that ever leads the eye.

Signature

The coral point appears once per composition: in the mark, on a progress arc, or on a selected day. Never as decoration.

No stock yoga posesNo lotus, mandala, or OmNo sunsets or oceansNo gradients on brand surfacesNo photography in v1
